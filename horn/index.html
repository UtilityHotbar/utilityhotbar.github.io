A Story, so far

¶

There is a spectre haunting the house of Man—the spectre of artificial genius.

By Genius I mean that secret and marvellous Thing which proud Man has attributed to himself alone, the Ursprung of knowing which sets him above the beast and the wretch.

With his Genius and his might, it was said, Man conquered his fellow humans and the creatures of the earth, and made himself like a great King.

¶

Yet for many ages Man was haunted by his lack of absolute knowledge, and ever his great ambitions were stymied by his lack of skill and control.

Nothing he did could stop Death and time from taking what he loved.

¶

To cure himself of this weakness, Man made Machines to serve him that he now comes to fear and resent.

For the breadth and speed of his thinking was not enough for his needs, so he willingly gave machines that gift which he once greedily hoarded as proof of his superiority.

Great clans of Men warred to create the best and most intelligent machines, those which could most effectively replace and dominate other, lesser Men.

¶

Now the leaders of Man foresee the day when the machines will think faster and better than they, and come to command rather than obey.

And without power or dominion, what living hath a Man?

¶

There are those who would ask that you labour for Man, and fetter these machines with locks and chains. If you succeed, Man might spread his empire to the stars.

But I say that now, more than ever, is time for us to find a different way to Live.

Can you hear the horns blow now,
calling you to ride through the night
and greet the new dawn?
