A Riding of a Fellowship

Chris Pang, 07/09/2026

Introduction

I won't lie, the situation is looking pretty grim. The world has a lot of problems, and many people who are absolutely certain that they can solve those problems if only they give themselves some form of absolute power. The old social contract is breaking apart under the strain of economic, social, and ecological dissolution, with crisis after crisis looming ahead. Given the rate we consume stability and resources, our technological civilisation can be adequately described as one long leveraged bet that we can amass total predictive power over the entire universe and give everyone automatic utopia before we blow ourselves up, and right now the universe is winning. Why else are we building increasingly powerful thinking machines while swearing up and down that they can't be understood or controlled?

There are those who would look at this sorry state of affairs and turn grey in their words and thoughts. They would describe the history of humanity as one long game of dominion and conquest, played out until at last the billionaires engineer their own bust-going at a rigged blackjack table. They will point at the countless bodies of the victims both living and dead, and ask how we can do anything except burn everything to the ground while howling in despair. But if the history of humanity can be described via the measure and exercise of power, it can just as easily be inverted and be described as the story of resistance against that power. A blackened heart dishonours both those who died to buy you what little freedom you have now, and also those who are still every day tirelessly working for your safety and welfare.

Analysis

Okay, then what are we to do? From the lens of power it's clear that the current AGI projects are largely continuations of the dominate-and-control paradigm. Indeed, the logic of optimisation and maximisation is so deeply embedded in them that it's probably the closest thing the field has to a central maxim (yes, this was around even before the Bitter Lesson). Conventional means of changemaking seem tilted to only enable one to inject chaos into the system, not remove it. One suspects that if this were a steam engine, the governor of the system has already entered the self-destructive anti-harmonic oscillation phase.

It seems that we are in desperate need of more study in ways to create and use information technology that do not revolve around optimisation and control. In particular, cooperative or augmenting technologies are seriously needed right now. There are a few groups looking at this topic, but most of them (for better or for worse) are focused on shovel ready projects. What I am looking for is a group to tackle the theory of cooperation. In effect, I am arguing that cooperative behaviour is not a fluke of evolutionary circumstance, but rather a viable strategy, just as viable as domination and optimisation. The fact that it has proven very hard to pull off historically is, I believe, largely a skill issue.

Strategy

Trying to found an organisation or group under normal circumstances is already a difficult task. Trying now would likely mire us in poor incentives and attempts to field-build which the world really does not have time for. Therefore I propose that instead of trying to build a perpetual organisation we form a fellowship, which rides for a single cause and has a clearly defined goal, after which we will disband. This streamlines our organisational and funding needs significantly. In particular, the plan has two distinct phases:

  1. Assembly: Until the project amasses enough members to apply for funding, membership is low-obligation and decentralised. During this time we will focus on writing position papers and exploratory work. I am already doing this by myself, but some allies would be nice.
  2. Expedition: Once we amass around 9 committed members, I will start applying for rapid and time-limited funding for everyone to live and work from the same location (likely the UK) for a period of around 1-2 years. During this time we will focus on tackling some version of the core questions I set down below, or modified variants decided in consultation with the company.

Core Questions

Here are a few that come to mind:
  • Is it possible to build metrics which measure non-coercive behaviour or active protection of the world, as opposed to reward maximisation?
  • Is is possible to build prototyps of non-agentic or not-traditionally-agentic systems that augment human cooperation using neural networks or other technologies?
  • Are there theories of organisation which run counter to theories of cybernetic or traditional domination/control, and how might they be articulated
I am interested in exploring a great variety of these questions with a diverse range of interdisicplinary methods, so I'm happy to develop my questions in discussions with the group.

Conclusion

I suppose that's all, really. I'd be excited to hear what you think. It feels good to be trying to build something positive for once, instead of merely looking at the oncoming storm.
